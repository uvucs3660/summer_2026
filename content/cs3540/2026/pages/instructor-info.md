## Why that matters to you

**The Soul, Sovereign, and Council material in this course is not theory.** It is a governance system running in production, with deliberations, votes, and a precedent library. When Module 3 says a decision log that records only winners cannot tell you why an option was rejected, that is a lesson learned by having to go back and ask.

Same with the rest. The MCP artifact you build in the Forge track is the thing I build for a living. The "declined versus blocked" distinction exists because I needed a hook to stop an agent doing something a prompt had already asked it not to do.

The 3D real-time strategy game this course is built around was written the same way you will write yours — one prompt, six answered questions — which is why I know how long it takes and where it breaks.

## How to get unstuck

In roughly this order:

1. **The cheat sheet.** Thirty-nine of them, each the 20% you need 80% of the time.
2. **The class spec.** `spec/S00-overview.md` answers most "how is this supposed to work" questions.
3. **A classmate.** You are all writing sections of one document; someone else has hit this.
4. **Me.** Bring what you tried. "It doesn't work" is hard to help with; "I expected X, got Y, and here's the command" takes two minutes.

## A note on the AI

You will spend this term working with a collaborator that is confidently wrong some of the time. That is not a flaw in the tool, it is the condition of the work now — and learning to verify rather than trust is most of what separates a productive 2026 engineer from an unproductive one.

When it gives you something you do not understand, stop. Shipping code you cannot explain is how you fail the Game Technique talk, and it is how people fail in industry.
