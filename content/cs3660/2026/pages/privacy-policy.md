# Grade Privacy & FERPA

Your grades and grade rationales are protected educational records under FERPA (Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, 20 U.S.C. § 1232g).

## Default rule

Every student's grade and rationale is **private**. You see only your own. The instructor and the LLM grader will **never** disclose your grade to a teammate, classmate, or anyone other than authorized university officials.

## Self-disclosure is your right

You may share your own grade or rationale with anyone you choose. The instructor neither facilitates nor obstructs this. There's no "share with team" feature in Canvas — sharing is a normal social act and your call.

## Visible work is not a grade

Repos, commits, pull requests, code-review comments, and demo videos are visible to your team because you collaborate on them. That's collaboration, not grade disclosure. In-sprint accountability happens via this visible work — not by reading after-the-fact grades.

## Aggregate-only public metrics

Class-level statistics (means, distributions) may be shared without student names. Per-team aggregate metrics may be shared with that team only, without per-individual breakdown.

## No structured peer evaluation

This course does not collect or distribute teammate-on-teammate evaluations. (Considered during design and rejected — the simpler default-private model is preferred.)

## LLM grader rationale style

The LLM grader's rationales are written so they could be safely forwarded to a teammate without revealing FERPA-protected information about other students. They reference *work* (commits, PRs, files), not *people by name*; describe contributions in scope language ("your work was concentrated on the persistence layer"), not comparative language ("you were the strongest member"); and use roles when describing surrounding work ("the integrator handled deploy"), not names.

## Concerns

If you believe your grade or rationale references another student inappropriately, or if you have any privacy concern about how grades are handled, contact the instructor directly.
